Ballast — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 August 2026

Ballast is a personal journalling app. It is designed to keep your writing on your own device. There is no Ballast account, no Ballast server, and no Ballast backend. Journal entries are not sent to the developer.

What stays on your device

Your journal entries and your activity list are stored as ordinary files in a BallastData folder on your device. Ballast does not automatically upload or sync them. Nothing in the app reads your entries and sends them to the developer.

If you choose "Back up to Google Drive," Ballast creates a ZIP containing the original entry files and activity settings, then opens your device's system save picker. The backup is written only to the destination you select. The chosen storage provider governs that transfer; the Ballast developer cannot access the backup.

What can leave your device

The in-app feedback form transmits data only when you explicitly tap submit. Ballast creates an issue in the developer's private issue tracker on GitHub containing:

DataWhy
The title and description you typedSo the bug or request can be understood and acted on
The app versionTo know which build the report concerns
Your browser/OS user-agent stringTo reproduce platform-specific problems
An optional screenshot you selectedTo show the problem you chose to report

Screenshots are optional, previewed before submission, and stored in a dedicated private branch of the Ballast GitHub repository. Nothing else is attached — no journal entries, no activity data, no logs, no device identifiers, no advertising ID, no location. Feedback requests go to api.github.com over HTTPS and to no other host. Ballast's direct feedback requests are restricted at the operating-system level to that single address.

This feature also requires you to supply your own GitHub access token before it will do anything, so it is inert unless you have deliberately configured it. That token is stored in the app's private configuration area on your device, is never written into the BallastData folder, and is sent only to GitHub as an authorisation header. You can remove it at any time from the "More" screen.

Update checks

When you tap "Update Ballast," the Google Play in-app update service checks whether a newer eligible version is available. Google Play receives device metadata, the installed app version, and the list of installed modules or asset packs for this purpose. Google states that this data is encrypted, is not transferred to third parties, and is deleted after a fixed retention period. Google Play's terms and privacy policy govern this processing; the Ballast developer does not receive this data.

What the app never does

  • No analytics, telemetry, or usage tracking
  • No crash or error reporting to any service
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers
  • No analytics or advertising SDKs
  • No accounts, sign-in, or profiles
  • No selling or sharing of data — there is no data held to sell

Children

Ballast is not directed at children and collects nothing that would identify anyone except for information a person deliberately includes in an optional feedback report.

Deleting your data

Your entries are yours and local: delete the BallastData folder, or individual files inside it, and they are gone. Note that on some platforms this folder lives outside the app's own storage, so uninstalling Ballast may leave it in place — delete it directly if you want it removed.

If you submitted feedback and want it deleted, email the address below and the corresponding issue and optional screenshot will be removed.
